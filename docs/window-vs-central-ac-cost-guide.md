# Window Unit vs Central AC: A Cost Breakdown for South Florida Homeowners

Every summer, South Florida homeowners face the same decision: stick with window units or invest in central air conditioning. Both options cool your home, but the long-term costs in our hot, humid climate differ dramatically.

This guide breaks down the real numbers so you can make an informed choice.

## The Upfront Cost Difference

**Window Units**
- Single unit: $300–$800 (installed)
- Cooling a 1,500 sq ft home: 4–5 units needed
- Total upfront: **$1,200–$4,000**
- No professional installation required
- Buy as you go — spread the cost over time

**Central AC**
- Complete system + installation: $5,500–$12,500
- Ductwork (if not existing): $2,500–$6,000
- Total upfront: **$8,000–$18,500**
- Requires licensed [Central AC installation costs in Miami-Dade](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-installation/) vary by home size and existing ductwork

Central AC costs 4–5× more upfront. But the story changes when you factor in operating costs and lifespan.

## Monthly Operating Costs in South Florida

South Florida's 8-month cooling season is the real budget killer. Here's what you'll actually spend running each system:

| Home Size | Window Units (monthly) | Central AC (monthly) |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| 1,000 sq ft (2 BR) | $180–$250 | $110–$145 |
| 1,500 sq ft (3 BR) | $280–$380 | $160–$210 |
| 2,000 sq ft (4 BR) | $400–$520 | $210–$280 |
| 2,500 sq ft (5 BR) | $520–$680 | $260–$340 |

*Based on FPL rates ($0.114/kWh) and 8 months/year of active cooling.*

**Why window units cost more to run:**

1. **Lower SEER ratings** — Most window units are SEER 10–12. Modern central systems start at SEER 14 (minimum) and go up to SEER 25+.
2. **Multiple compressors** — Five window units running = five compressors consuming power independently. One central unit with a variable-speed compressor uses far less total energy.
3. **Poor insulation** — Window units leak cool air around the mounting frame, especially in older South Florida homes with jalousie windows.
4. **No dehumidification** — Central ACs remove 30–50% more humidity per kWh, so the thermostat can be set higher while feeling equally comfortable.

A central system running at 78°F with good dehumidification feels cooler than a window unit at 74°F with high humidity.

## Lifespan and Replacement Costs

**Window Units**
- Average lifespan: 4–6 years (8–10 if well-maintained)
- Salt air from coastal areas reduces lifespan to 3–5 years
- Replacement: rebuy the same unit every 5 years
- 20-year cost: **$4,800–$16,000** in replacement units alone

**Central AC**
- Average lifespan: 12–15 years
- With [professional AC maintenance in South Florida](https://ac-repair.today/services/ac-maintenance/): 15–18 years
- 20-year cost: 1 replacement + 1 major repair ≈ **$7,000–$14,000**

## Hidden Costs Most Homeowners Miss

### Window Unit Hidden Costs
- **Structural wear**: Window units strain window frames. In older South Florida homes (concrete block construction), the weight and vibration can cause frame damage over time.
- **Security risk**: Units can be pushed in from outside. Each unit is a potential entry point.
- **Aesthetic impact**: Multiple units sticking out of windows reduce property value.
- **Noise pollution**: Indoor noise levels of 50–60 dB per unit. Five units = constant background hum.
- **Condensation damage**: Poor drainage leads to water stains on walls and sills — a mold risk in our humid climate.

### Central AC Hidden Savings
- **Home value increase**: Central AC adds 5–10% to resale value in South Florida.
- **Better air filtration**: Whole-home filtration captures pollen, mold spores, and dust — critical during South Florida's allergy seasons.
- **Quiet operation**: Indoor noise at 25–35 dB. You forget it's running.
- **Even cooling**: No hot rooms or cold rooms. Every room stays within 2°F of the thermostat setpoint.
- **Smart thermostat integration**: Programmable schedules, remote control, and usage tracking can cut another 10–15% off cooling costs.

## The 5-Year Breakeven Point

Here's the simple math for a typical 1,500 sq ft South Florida home:

| Cost Category | Window Units (5 units) | Central AC |
|---------------|----------------------|------------|
| Upfront cost | $3,000 | $10,500 |
| Annual energy cost | $3,960 | $2,220 |
| Annual maintenance | $200 | $300 |
| 5-year total | **$23,800** | **$22,200** |

Central AC reaches breakeven between year 3 and 5 in most South Florida homes. After that, the savings accelerate.

For a 2,000 sq ft home, breakeven happens even faster — closer to year 3 — because the energy cost gap widens with more square footage.

## When Window Units Actually Make Sense

Window units aren't always the wrong choice. They make sense when:

1. **Renting** — You can't modify the property. Take the units with you when you move.
2. **Cooling one room** — A bedroom or home office. One unit = $40–60/month vs adding a whole-house system.
3. **Short-term living** — You'll move within 2 years. Recouping central AC investment takes 3+ years.
4. **No ductwork and no attic space** — Some South Florida condos and townhouses have nowhere to run ducts.

## Our Recommendation for South Florida Homeowners

If you own your home and plan to stay 3+ years, **central AC is the better investment** despite the higher upfront cost. The monthly savings, comfort improvement, and home value increase more than justify the difference.

If you're not ready for a full installation, consider a phased approach:
1. Start with a mini-split system for the main living area (lower upfront, SEER 18–30)
2. Add zones as budget allows
3. Eventually replace with a full central system
